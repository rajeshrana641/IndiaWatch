Supreme Court acquits father-in-law under 498A — rules "afterthought testimonies" and "gimmick contradictions" cannot sustain conviction
Acquittal⚖ Supreme Court of IndiaJustice J.K. Maheshwari & Justice Atul S. Chandurkar
  • Bench set aside conviction of father-in-law under Section 498A IPC, holding prosecution evidence failed the test of reliability due to material improvements in testimonies.
  • Court observed that while Section 498A is a vital tool for women's safety, it must not be misused to rope in all family members without specific, attributable roles.
  • Testimonies described as "afterthought" additions — court found significant contradictions between trial court statement and appellate testimony.
  • Citation: 2026 INSC 439 — sets precedent requiring courts to scrutinise each family member's specific alleged role before sustaining conviction.

Why don't retail prices fall when crude falls?

► Excise + VAT = ~55% of petrol price creates a price floor — any fall in crude is absorbed by OMC margins, not passed to consumers.
