Supreme Court acquits father-in-law under 498A — rules "afterthought testimonies" and "gimmick contradictions" cannot sustain conviction
Acquittal⚖ Supreme Court of IndiaJustice J.K. Maheshwari & Justice Atul S. Chandurkar
  • Bench set aside conviction of father-in-law under Section 498A IPC, holding prosecution evidence failed the test of reliability due to material improvements in testimonies.
  • Court observed that while Section 498A is a vital tool for women's safety, it must not be misused to rope in all family members without specific, attributable roles.
  • Testimonies described as "afterthought" additions — court found significant contradictions between trial court statement and appellate testimony.
  • Citation: 2026 INSC 439 — sets precedent requiring courts to scrutinise each family member's specific alleged role before sustaining conviction.

Supreme Court issues notice to Centre, RBI & 7 banks over ₹22.9 crore "digital arrest" scam — 82-year-old defrauded in India's largest individual cyber fraud
Cyber Fraud⚖ Supreme Court of IndiaCJI Surya Kant & Justice Joymalya Bagchi
  • Bench took suo motu cognisance after 82-year-old senior citizen lost ₹22.92 crore to fraudsters impersonating CBI and RBI officials — possibly the largest individual cyber fraud in India's history.

Recusal controversy: SC judge declines to recuse from case involving company where family member holds directorship Ongoing
Conflict of InterestSupreme Court of India
  • Bar Association of India filed representation citing conflict of interest; Judge acknowledged the connection but said no bias would result.
  • India has no statutory disclosure requirement for judges' financial interests or family connections to litigants.
  • UK, USA, and Australia mandate annual asset declarations and mandatory recusal on family conflict; India relies on judicial discretion alone.
  • RTI applications for judges' asset declarations consistently rejected by SC citing "judicial independence" — no external audit mechanism exists.

Why don't retail prices fall when crude falls?

► Excise + VAT = ~55% of petrol price creates a price floor — any fall in crude is absorbed by OMC margins, not passed to consumers.

► Iran War 2026 spike: Brent hit $90+ in May; 4 hikes in 2 weeks added ~₹8/L on petrol, ₹7/L on diesel.

► Covid excise grab (2020): Centre hiked excise by ₹13/L when crude was $19/bbl. Never reversed.

Bottom Line: India's fuel price is a political instrument, not a market price.
