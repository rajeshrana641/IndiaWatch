Why don't retail petrol prices fall when crude oil falls?

▸ Excise duty & state VAT account for ~55–58% of the retail petrol price (central excise ≈ ₹19.90/L + state VAT ≈ ₹15–19/L), creating a price floor that absorbs downward crude movements.

▸ OMC margin buffering: Oil Marketing Companies (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL) under-recover during crude spikes and over-recover during dips — the government prefers stable prices over pass-through.

▸ Excise hike during Covid-19 (2020): Centre raised excise by ₹13/L in May 2020 when crude crashed to $19/barrel — consumers never saw the benefit.

▸ Price freeze of 2022: Despite Brent surging past $105/barrel, government froze retail prices for 137 days. OMCs reported combined losses of ₹18,480 crore in Q1 FY23.

▸ Iran War fuel hikes (2026): Petrol price raised 4 times in 2 weeks following Iran conflict-driven crude spike; Delhi petrol crossed ₹100/L for first time since 2022.

Bottom Line: India's retail fuel prices are a political instrument, not a market price.

5-Year Middle-Class Tax Trend · FY2020–FY2025

▸ Total tax burden rose ~32% from FY2020 to FY2025 for a ₹12L income household, driven by GST rationalisation, sustained high fuel excise, and the compounding 4% cess.

▸ New Tax Regime (FY2024 onwards) offers lower slab rates but eliminates ₹1.5L 80C, HRA, and LTA deductions — effectively benefiting only those with minimal investments.

▸ Indirect taxes dominate: A middle-class household pays ₹60,000–₹90,000/year in GST alone on goods and services, often unnoticed as it is embedded in prices.

Bottom Line: India's middle-class taxpayer has no lobby, no subsidy, and no escape — bearing the highest effective burden relative to income of any demographic group.
