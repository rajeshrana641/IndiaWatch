Crude Oil vs Retail Fuel Prices in India · 5-Year Tracker
Brent Crude (USD/barrel, left axis) vs Delhi Petrol Retail Price (₹/litre, right axis) · Jan 2020 – Jan 2025
Why don't retail petrol prices fall when crude oil falls?

▸ Excise duty & state VAT account for ~55–58% of the retail petrol price (central excise ≈ ₹19.90/L + state VAT ≈ ₹15–19/L), creating a price floor that absorbs downward crude movements.

▸ OMC margin buffering: Oil Marketing Companies (IOCL, BPCL, HPCL) under-recover during crude spikes and over-recover during dips — the government prefers stable prices over pass-through to avoid inflation headlines.

▸ Excise hike during Covid-19 (2020): Centre raised excise by ₹13/L in May 2020 when crude crashed to $19/barrel — consumers never saw the benefit; instead revenue was captured into Consolidated Fund.

▸ Price freeze of 2022: Despite Brent surging past $105/barrel, the government froze retail prices for 137 days before a ₹10/L hike. OMCs reported combined losses of ₹18,480 crore in Q1 FY23.

Bottom Line: India's retail fuel prices are a political instrument, not a market price — taxes cushion the government from crude volatility in both directions, at the consumer's expense.

5-Year Middle-Class Tax Trend · FY2020–FY2025

▸ Total tax burden rose ~32% from FY2020 to FY2025 for a ₹12L income household, driven by GST rationalisation, sustained high fuel excise, and the compounding 4% cess.

▸ New Tax Regime (FY2024 onwards) offers lower slab rates but eliminates ₹1.5L 80C, HRA, and LTA deductions — effectively benefiting only those with minimal investments and rented housing.

▸ Indirect taxes dominate: A middle-class household pays ₹60,000–₹90,000/year in GST alone on goods and services (cars, electronics, eating out, insurance), often unnoticed as it is embedded in prices.

▸ Fuel excise windfall: Centre collected ₹3.35 lakh crore in fuel excise in FY2023 alone — largely borne by the salaried middle class through commuting and logistics cost pass-through.

Bottom Line: India's middle-class taxpayer has no lobby, no subsidy, and no escape — bearing the highest effective burden relative to income of any demographic group.
